The problem

A speaker in an open-plan office is a hostile act.

An elevation desk at full height is a stage. A speaker on it is an incident. Somewhere behind you, someone is trying to think about a spreadsheet. Floorfill lets you dance anyway. Your music, your floor, everyone's own volume knob.

How it works

Cast. Clock. Headphones.

Three steps, two of which happen without you.

1

Cast

AirPlay from your iPhone or Mac, or pick Floorfill in Spotify's device list from any phone. Music, Spotify, Tidal, podcasts. Anything that can cast.

2

Clock

Your Mac stamps every twenty milliseconds of music against one clock and hands it to whoever asks. We call it the clock. It's one of our better names.

3

Headphones

Colleagues open one address in a browser and press play. They hear it together. The page shows the track and the cover art. Nothing to install. Nothing to explain to IT.

The breakthrough

Introducing the Sync Engine.

Every listener's browser measures its distance to your Mac's clock, dozens of times a minute, and schedules each chunk of audio to play at the agreed instant. Drift past five milliseconds and it corrects itself. Twenty people, one downbeat. Industry-leading togetherness.

It's Snapcast. It has been doing this for living rooms for a decade. We pointed it at an office.

Features. A reasonable number of features.

Our most complete Floorfill ever.

Also our first.

Sync. Actual sync.

Laptops land within a few tens of milliseconds of each other. Nobody can hear anyone else's headphones, so that is a rounding error with a beat.

Zero install

A web page. The most compatible app we have ever shipped. Works on the laptop IT gave you and the phone IT doesn't know about.

Cover art. Large.

Title, artist, album, artwork. The page answers "what is this?" before anyone has to take their headphones off to ask.

Two ways in

AirPlay for iPhones and Macs. Spotify Connect for every phone with Spotify. Both land on the same floor. Chromecast? See Honesty, below.

Privacy. On the LAN.

Nothing leaves the office. Not the music, not the listener list, not your taste in nineties house. No account, no cloud, no telemetry.

Your laptop is the venue

One Mac, awake, on the network. Close the lid and the night is over. Some would call that a limitation. We call it closing time.

Honesty. A courageous feature.

Three things we would rather tell you now.

Chromecast. We tried.

Spotify and YouTube check that a Cast receiver carries a certificate signed by Google. Nobody outside Google can get one, so no open-source receiver passes. Spotify Connect covers most of what people want Chromecast for. If you truly need one, a physical Chromecast with audio out into your Mac works, minus the cover art.

Spotify needs Premium.

librespot, the Spotify Connect receiver Floorfill uses, is firm about it. AirPlay works with whatever you already pay for, including nothing.

Phones listen with the screen on.

iOS stops a web page's audio when the screen locks. Laptops are the better listener. Phones are the better DJ. This arrangement suits everyone.

The experience

Press play. Put them on. Lose the afternoon.

Listeners get their own volume, the current track, and a quiet indicator of how far they are from the clock. DJs get a device in the AirPlay list, like every other speaker they have ever used. Except this one is everyone.

Available today

Not in a shop. On brew.

Four lines on the DJ's Mac. Zero lines on anyone else's.

brew install den-frie-vilje/tap/floorfill
floorfill init          # checks the parts, fetches the listener page
floorfill start         # runs until you say stop
floorfill url           # the address to send round

Then AirPlay to "Floorfill" and send the address round. The price? There isn't one. Courage.

One more thing

It's one Python file. And three giants.

Snapcast keeps the clock. shairport-sync receives AirPlay. librespot receives Spotify Connect. Snapweb plays it in the browser. We wrote the bit that introduces them to each other. You can read all of it before lunch.
