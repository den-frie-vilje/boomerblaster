Do you remember the time

When the office was the party.

You lived under your desk. It was the dot-com boom, and the desk was where the boom happened. You smoked indoors. You ripped your vinyl to 128 kbit/s and put the vinyl out with the recycling.

Everyone was noisy. Everyone listened to the same thing, because everyone had to. Noise cancelling meant someone going schhh. And every week somebody nudged the volume knob one notch, and nobody noticed, and the frog boiled.

Well. No?

Now you can at least listen together again. And embarrass yourself with your low-BPM downtempo2 in front of the whole floor, at a volume that bothers no one.

Same room. Same record. Separate ears.

How it works

Cast. Clock. Headphones.

Three steps. You do the first. Your Mac does the second. Your colleagues do the third, and they get to decide how loud.

1

Cast

AirPlay from your iPhone or Mac, or pick BoomerBlaster in Spotify's device list from any phone. Music, Spotify, Tidal, podcasts. Anything with a cast button.

2

Clock

Your Mac stamps every twenty milliseconds of music against one clock. Every listener's browser measures its distance to that clock and plays each chunk at the agreed instant. Drift past five milliseconds and it corrects itself.3

3

Headphones

Colleagues open one address, press play, put their headphones on. Cover art, track, artist, their own volume. Nothing to install. Nothing to explain to IT.

Tech Specs

Requirements

A Mac with Homebrew. A network where devices can reach each other. Headphones for everyone.

Listeners: any browser on a laptop; phones with the screen on.7

Sources

AirPlay (classic), via shairport-sync.

Spotify Connect, via librespot. Spotify Premium required.

Sync

Snapcast: server-stamped 20 ms chunks, client clock estimation, hard correction beyond 5 ms.

Audio

44.1 kHz, 16-bit, stereo. FLAC by default; PCM or Opus by setting.

About 700 kbit/s per listener with FLAC.

Latency

About two seconds from source to ears, by AirPlay's design.

Ports

1780 listener page and control, 1704 and 1705 Snapcast, 5000 AirPlay.

Metadata

Title, artist, album and cover art from AirPlay sources that send them. Title and artist from Spotify Connect.

Privacy

Nothing leaves the local network. No account, no cloud, no telemetry.

In the box

One Python file, standard library only. Fetches Snapweb 0.9.3 on first run and verifies its checksum.

Not in the box

Chromecast.6 Indoor smoking. Your vinyl.

  1. Requires Homebrew on the DJ's Mac. The dependencies snapcast, shairport-sync and librespot install with it. Office networks that isolate clients prevent colleagues from reaching the page; ask IT, nicely.
  2. Downtempo generally sits below 100 beats per minute. Your colleagues can see the cover art. Choose accordingly.
  3. Based on Snapcast's browser client. Browsers that report their output latency precisely land within a few tens of milliseconds of each other; Safari may sit slightly further off. Tested on an office floor with headphones, where nobody can hear anyone else's drift.
  4. AirPlay buffers approximately two seconds by design. Beat-matching across the office is not supported and not recommended.
  5. Spotify Connect through librespot requires a Spotify Premium account. librespot is firm about it. AirPlay works with whatever you already pay for, including nothing.
  6. Spotify and YouTube check that a Cast receiver carries a certificate signed by Google. Nobody outside Google can get one, so no open-source receiver passes. Spotify Connect covers most of what people want Chromecast for. A physical Chromecast with audio out into the Mac works, minus the cover art.
  7. iOS stops a web page's audio when the screen locks. Laptops are the better listener. Phones are the better DJ. The arrangement suits everyone, and it is also the only one available.
